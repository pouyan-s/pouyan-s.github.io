		  <h4 style="color:#8ED530">Overview</h4>
		  <p>In my doctoral dissertation, I employed GIS-based mapping as a digital humanities method to engage with the complex corpus of Avicenna (Ibn Sīnā, 10th–11th c. CE), much of which was produced during his lifelong itinerancy. I extracted his whereabouts — recorded as placenames with latitude and longitude — and his scholarly output across time and space, structuring this data as a CSV. I also digitized roads and the shifting borders of ruling dynasties during Avicenna’s lifetime from historical print maps. </p><p> These data layers were visualized in a time-enabled map using ArcGIS Pro, then published to ArcGIS Online and converted into a web-based map application via ArcGIS Instant Apps. I further used this dataset to conduct geospatial, quantitative analyses in ArcGIS Insights, revealing where, when, and how intensely Avicenna was active in writing on subdisciplines of physics and mathematical astronomy.</p>
        <!-- List of deliverables -->
		<h4 style="color:#8ED530">Project deliverables include</h4>
		<ol>
			<li><a href="https://iu.maps.arcgis.com/apps/instant/slider/index.html?appid=3923df36605840ca82dfb64d3090db59" target="_blank">An animated survey map of Avicenna’s datable works</a></li>
			<li><a href="https://insights.arcgis.com/#/view/cdc61d577e694380a83d3b1fbb62730e" target="_blank">A map-graph tool (ArcGIS Insights workbook) visualizing genre-specific scholarship across place</a></li>
			<li>A geodatabase of the works, designed for reshaping, expansion, and reuse by historians interested in Avicenna’s corpus or the historical context of his era</li>
		</ol>
		<!-- Software & data-->
		<h4 style="color:#8ED530">Software</h4><p>ArcGIS Pro 3.1, ArcGIS Online, ArcGIS Instant Apps, ArcGIS Insights</p>

		</ul>
